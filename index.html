Every day

non-negotiable

These four move the needle more than any single session. Resets each morning.

Run the session

same every time

The structure doesn't change — only the exercises do.

  1. Five minutes on a bike or rower to raise the temperature, then ten squats, ten press-ups, ten band pull-aparts.
  2. First set of each exercise is a feeler at about 60% of your working weight. It doesn't count.
  3. Working sets stop two reps short of failure. You should believe you could do two more, every set.
  4. Rest 60–75 seconds. Set a timer — everyone drifts to three minutes without one.
  5. Same weight all sets. If the last set feels easy, add weight next session, not this one.
  6. Finish with the ten-minute thoracic block. Don't skip it because you're hungry.

Whole thing should take 45–55 minutes. If it's running past an hour you're resting too long, not working too hard.

Rules, not calories. Every main meal gets built in this order — veg first, then protein, then the starch.

Don't overdo the deficit

Eat a bit less, not a lot less — and don't fast. Rapid weight loss and dehydration both push urate up, and a gout flare on day two of the holiday costs you far more than a slightly softer stomach. If a joint starts niggling, eat normally and drink more.

Thoracic extension

free inches

Your thoracic spine is the middle twelve vertebrae — the ones your ribs hang off. Extension is arching back through that section.

Sitting, driving and pressing all pull you into the shape on the left. When the upper back rounds, the ribcage tips down toward the pelvis, the abdominal wall slackens and the front of you folds forward — a soft roll appears without a single gram of extra fat. Open the mid-back and the ribs stack over the hips, the waist lengthens, the stomach flattens.

The cue isn't "shoulders back." It's tall ribs over hips — grow through the sternum and let the shoulders sit where they land.

Heavy training in the final 48 hours leaves the abdominal wall inflamed and holding water — exactly the opposite of what you want on the beach. Back off and let it settle.

Realistic outcome by the 21st: around half a kilo to a kilo of actual fat, one to two kilos of water, and a noticeably flatter, tighter-looking midsection. The look changes more than the scale does.
